# Reading and writing progression

## Course structure

CEFR level → course half (for example A1.1 or A1.2) → topic unit → lessons, stories, writing, checkpoint.

Every topic unit has at least four short stories, spread across the unit. Current A1.1 ordering:

Discover → Story 1 → Build confidence → Story 2 → Use it → Story 3 → Write in Spanish → Checkpoint → Story 4.

Regular lessons and writing activities contain at least five questions. Each story has five comprehension questions: three multiple-choice and two short typed responses. The passage appears before the questions, without a countdown or forced delay. Learners decide when they are ready and may reread it during questions or spaced review.

## Level targets

| Stage | Story target | Comprehension response | Spanish production |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1.1 | Short present-tense sentences, familiar people and concrete facts | English or Spanish; words and short phrases | Introductions and single familiar sentences |
| A1.2 | Slightly longer everyday exchanges, basic descriptions and sequences | English or Spanish | Two linked sentences and simple exchanges |
| A2.1 | Familiar situations with simple past events and plans | English or Spanish | Short descriptions, questions, and past-event sentences |
| A2.2 | Connected short stories with simple reasons and comparisons | English or Spanish | Several connected sentences and simple messages |
| B1 | Longer familiar narratives, motivations and straightforward inference | Primarily Spanish | Short paragraphs and explanations |
| B2 | More nuanced viewpoints, inference and less explicit information | Spanish | Supported opinions and more detailed responses |

Later-stage targets are a content plan, not playable curriculum or CEFR certification. Only A1.1 activities are currently authored. Review vocabulary in each story as the later course is written; story length alone does not establish its level.

## Answer feedback

A1/A2 story questions support English or Spanish. Multiple-choice story options display both languages. Typed questions explicitly request a word or short phrase. Spanish-writing lessons ask for Spanish and use authored accepted variants. Matching tolerates case, spaces, punctuation and vowel accents while preserving ñ. This is finite answer matching; broader paraphrase assessment requires a separate grading design before longer free writing is introduced.

Story questions retain their passage when saved for spaced review. Existing lesson IDs and completion records remain valid when new stories and writing activities are added.
