## Unit structure and communication

### B1.1

1. **Telling the story** — Narrate an unexpected event with background, sequence and outcome Grammar support: Past integration; primero, mientras, de repente, al final.
2. **Things that have changed** — Compare past and present habits and explain how long a change has lasted Grammar support: Antes/ahora, ya no, todavía, desde, desde hace, hace… que.
3. **Plans and intentions** — Explain plans, arrangements and future commitments Grammar support: Simple future iré, tendré, haré, podré; ir a; present arrangements.
4. **Advice and solutions** — Offer practical advice while respecting the other person’s choice Grammar support: Conditional advice deberías/podrías; yo que tú; no hace falta; obligation.
5. **Experiences and reactions** — Describe an experience and explain a personal reaction Grammar support: Me sorprendió/preocupó/encantó; me pareció; lo pasé; tenía ganas de.
6. **Explaining your view** — Express a view, support it and acknowledge a different advantage Grammar support: Creo/pienso/me parece que; por una parte; sin embargo; por eso; por/para.
7. **When things go wrong** — Clarify a less predictable service problem and negotiate a practical solution Grammar support: Communication repair; delivery/booking/lost-property problems; polite clarification.
8. **A week to remember** — Sustain a short connected account integrating events, reactions, reasons and changed plans Grammar support: B1.1 synthesis: tense meaning, future, conditional advice, viewpoint.

### B1.2

1. **People and relationships** — Explain an interpersonal misunderstanding and agree a constructive response Grammar support: Llevarse bien, confiar en, misunderstandings; por/para consolidation.
2. **Work and study** — Explain responsibilities, experience and a reasoned work/study decision Grammar support: Responsibilities, applications, collaboration; desde/experience; clear formal requests.
3. **Media and technology** — Explain technology habits, weigh benefits and resolve a familiar digital problem Grammar support: Instructions, se puede; advantages/disadvantages; reasons and clarification.
4. **Health and wellbeing** — Explain a health history and daily habits, and seek appropriate help Grammar support: Symptoms over time; practical advice; avoiding medical claims.
5. **Travel and culture** — Recommend a visit with reasons, practical limits and cultural observations Grammar support: Experiences, comparisons, recommendations; avoiding generalisations.
6. **Hopes and hypotheticals** — Describe an imagined possibility and a desired change without presenting it as fact Grammar support: Conditional gustaría/sería/podría/haría/iría; hypothetical context without full si paradigms.
7. **Wishes, reactions and necessity** — Express a wish, reaction or necessity involving another person Grammar support: Limited present subjunctive after quiero que, espero que, es importante que, me alegra que; subject changes.
8. **Making your case** — Sustain an opinion with experience, a response to another view and a solution Grammar support: B1 synthesis; examples, reasons, polite response, proposals and limited subjunctive.

### B2.1

1. **Stories with detail** — Relate a detailed account while distinguishing chronology, background and viewpoint Grammar support: Past integration; limited pluperfect for a prior event; narrator perspective.
2. **Causes and consequences** — Explain a chain of causes and consequences without overstating what the evidence proves Grammar support: Debido a, a causa de, por lo tanto, como resultado; cause versus correlation.
3. **Certainty, doubt and possibility** — Separate what is known, believed and possible when explaining a situation Grammar support: Probablemente/quizá; puede que; es posible que; no creo que + present subjunctive.
4. **Giving and defending opinions** — Defend a position with qualification, a counterpoint and relevant support Grammar support: Polite disagreement; si bien, aunque, sin embargo; partial agreement; support scope.
5. **Formal everyday communication** — Write a clear enquiry or complaint with relevant evidence and a reasonable request Grammar support: Register; le agradecería; quisiera; formal structure; specific remedy.
6. **News, media and information** — Summarise a source accurately and distinguish fact, reported claim and viewpoint Grammar support: Source attribution; dice que/dijo que; direct versus reported information; no automatic tense backshift.
7. **Hypothetical situations** — Explain an imagined present alternative and its likely consequences Grammar support: Si + imperfect subjunctive; conditional result; real/open si + indicative.
8. **Discussing a problem** — Explain a problem, compare options and recommend a solution responsive to another view Grammar support: Integrated argument: cause, evidence, options, consequences, conditional proposal.

### B2.2

1. **Viewpoint and perspective** — Qualify a position, acknowledge another perspective and identify evidence limits Grammar support: Hasta cierto punto; desde mi punto de vista; no necesariamente; certainty and scope.
2. **Complex narration** — Produce an extended account linking earlier events, reactions, consequences and reflection Grammar support: Pluperfect and tense integration; conditional perfect reflection; chronology versus viewpoint.
3. **Argument and counterargument** — Develop a supported position, engage with an objection and qualify the conclusion Grammar support: Position, evidence, concession, counterargument and bounded conclusion.
4. **Social and cultural topics** — Discuss familiar social choices with evidence, perspectives and practical consequences Grammar support: Accessible social topics; generalisation, comparison and trade-offs.
5. **Formal and informal register** — Adapt a truthful message to its recipient without losing essential facts Grammar support: Recipient, greeting, request, degree of formality and closing.
6. **Reported speech and information** — Relay claims accurately, attribute perspectives and retain uncertainty Grammar support: Reporting verbs, source attribution, reference time and qualified paraphrase.
7. **Possibility, condition and consequence** — Explain real and imagined conditions, distinguish uncertainty and reflect on past alternatives Grammar support: Real/open conditions; imagined present and past; conditional perfect; limited perfect subjunctive.
8. **The B2 project** — Integrate narration, explanation, evidence, perspectives and a practical proposal for real recipients Grammar support: Integrated extended communication; revision, register and evidence limits.

## Writing resources, implementation and dependency decisions

Read the repository’s WRITING_ENGINE.md, writing-tools.json, CODEX_REFERENCE.md and existing pedagogical/provenance resources. Their variant-first, layered, offline and false-rejection principles are implemented in `writing.js`, `writing-rules.js`, `verb-diagnostics.js` and `writing-audit.js`.

No runtime dependency was added. Conjugator v2.1.0 (Apache-2.0) was evaluated: Node filesystem/path/url imports and JSON/TSV resource reads make direct WebView embedding unsafe. Its proposed adapter API is implemented using original course paradigms, not copied library data. Nspell (MIT) is deferred with dictionary-es because no GPL/LGPL/MPL redistribution path has been selected and unknown-name/regional-word diagnostics need evaluation. NLP.js (MIT) remains an experiment: an unvalidated intent score would not prove grammar. LanguageTool/Apertium remain references, not copied rules or heavier embedded runtimes. No third-party text, recording or dataset was added; no new third-party attribution notice is required.

Controlled tasks still use normalised exact variants and deterministic incorrect feedback. Guided tasks verify authored equivalent formulations; otherwise a detected high-confidence issue receives coaching, coverage markers support reflection, and uncertainty shows one possible model. Free writing never receives automatic verified correctness, even for an exact model match. Absence of a rule hit does not certify grammar. Empty/explicit authored off-task responses are handled conservatively; arbitrary intent is not guessed.

Twenty-three original rules cover age/hunger/thirst, me/mi names, llamarse, gustar number/structure, contractions, plans, reflexive routines, article/adjective agreement, location/state/origin, irregular participles, a narrowly authored por/para purpose, wishes/necessity/doubt and imagined si conditions. Rules wait until the appropriate stage/unit. Authored variants and specific misconceptions take precedence. Quoted errors, proper names and ambiguous el/tu article/pronoun uses receive safeguards.

The morphology adapter diagnoses known wrong-person and explicitly requested wrong-tense/mood forms. It keeps Castilian tú/vosotros and irregular/compound explanations, and accepts -ra/-se past-subjunctive alternatives. It does not discover arbitrary verb lemmas or grade whole sentences. Spelling support is limited near-model edit distance (at most two edits/500 characters), explicitly presented as comparison rather than a dictionary check. General spelling/intent grading is deliberately deferred rather than falsely advertised.

The false-rejection regression corpus contains **30 unexpected valid responses**, with names, alternative word order, formal/regional forms, negation, proper names, quoted errors, article/verb homographs and subjunctive alternatives. The audit also checks **480 new B Spanish models** against open-response diagnostics. This is a small first-party corpus, not a measured population false-rejection rate or native-speaker validation.

## Content quality and progression audit

All new source units/models, 128 stories, media adaptations, distractors, paradigms and accepted variants were inspected. Dedicated writing mode ordering was corrected to make the final, most independent task Free. Two B2 examples that introduced past counterfactual forms too early were simplified; that construction remains taught in B2.2 unit 7. Thirteen new glossary entries were aligned with the actual inflected phrase or useful passage word. Table-source checks removed/replaced speculative untaught targets and linked each target to an actual course model or source passage. Accent review fixed compound -se nosotros alternatives, including hubiésemos.

The sentence-bank audit caught noun/conjunction homographs (trabajo, reserva, reservas, como) and excluded those matches; shared yo/third-person forms no longer generate false person mastery. These corrections affect new draft B content only. Existing A1–A2 records are protected by complete per-activity hashes plus original permanent-ID manifests and historical fixtures.

B1 expands beyond A2 into connected narratives, consequences, change, recommendations, misunderstandings, arguments and limited wishes. B2 requires distinctions among facts, interpretations, uncertainty, source attribution, perspective, register and conditions. It is richer communication rather than simply longer text or obscure vocabulary. Reading uses context/cause/inference/viewpoint/response, not five factual questions. Listening requires increasingly connected interpretation and Speaking increasingly connected production; models remain one possibility, with no pronunciation verification.

Vocabulary is recycled through travel, neighbourhood, work, service, technology and group-planning contexts. [v1-vocabulary.json](v1-vocabulary.json) records unit coverage of positive models and unique stories, excluding distractors, English, checkpoint copies and repeated passage attachments. Surface forms are not lemmatised: exposure is not mastery, and one-off words may be useful glossary-supported scenario vocabulary. Frequent connectors and familiar verbs recur across stages. No automatic vocabulary replacement was made solely to improve a statistic.

The new story ranges fall below some approximate length suggestions, especially B1.2/B2.1. They retain complete situations, competing perspectives and supported interpretation; arbitrary padding was avoided. Educator/learner review must determine whether this depth and pacing is enough before wider distribution. The eight units per stage are complete course chapters, not claims of exhaustive CEFR coverage.

## Practice, assessment, navigation and product integration

Each new stage contributes 56 Listening + 56 Speaking items, adapted from original course clips, dialogues, narratives and production tasks. Normal ten-item sessions use recent-history avoidance, topic/category balance and weakness weights, normally six current plus earlier retrieval. B narrative/retelling items require their actual source story, so a learner cannot be asked to summarise an unknown passage. Other prerequisites remain taught core language; assumed placement foundations remain eligible. English support persists and resets per item, carrying weaker evidence. Listening remains locked until actual start; transcript concealment, replay/speed/failure/skip remain.

Conjugations adds 64 meaningful sentence contexts per B stage. Tables add 8 B1.1, 7 B1.2, 6 B2.1 and 6 B2.2 targets; all six persons and irregular/compound teaching metadata are included. Normal three-table games and 3/5/10/15/20 Practice retain random positions, adaptive weakness targeting, hints, typed recall, anti-repeat and mastery-gated full tables. The independent grammar-level ceiling remains separate from completion, as requested; mastery changes scaffolding, not access. Games never award course completion or alter placement/startStage.

New checkpoints contain five objective items (recognition, controlled completion, listening, reading, dialogue) and four open responses. Four of five objectively correct meets the unchanged 80% threshold, with all reflections required. Their final task is a new synthesis for someone unfamiliar with the unit’s final scenario, rather than a duplicate writing prompt. Repetition cannot duplicate capabilities. No free-writing confidence is misrepresented as verified accuracy.

The provisional eight-question placement can recommend playable B material but cannot establish B speaking/writing proficiency; B2.2 remains manually selectable. All eight stages are navigable. The final B2 milestone reports actual completions, capabilities and assumed foundations accurately; review/listening/speaking maintenance remains, with no C1/C2 offer and no CEFR certification. Today, Today’s Review, Weak Areas, main Continue/retry, Android Back, keyboard/safe areas, dark mode and bull identity remain integrated.

## Performance and engineering review

The app remains vanilla offline JavaScript, with separate static B data, a small builder and build-time adaptations; no framework or service was introduced. Runtime content does not generate exercises. Storage/course lookups and recommendation contexts cache indexes to avoid repeatedly traversing the expanded course. Level changes invalidate recommendation caches.

On the Linux Node execution workspace, the latest ten-iteration diagnostic with all completed lessons, 200 review snapshots and a saved B2 story measured module loading 174.56 ms, Listening selection 6.99 ms, sentence rounds 5.29 ms, tables 4.23 ms, validated import 3.22 ms and heap usage 32.14 MiB. Values vary with machine/cache load and are not S24/browser performance claims. Web assets total about 3.5 MiB; no audio corpus was added. Static data duplication keeps the implementation simple but should be profiled on a real device before any future lazy-loading refactor.

## Validation, limitations and next decision

The full validation workflow [37644212496](https://github.com/mmwiza1/Friends-to-Amigos/actions/runs/37644212496) passed: **649 JS cases** (0 failed/cancelled/skipped/todo), **13,981 rendered assertions across 11 layouts**, Android debug build, **lint 0 errors / 12 unchanged warnings**, and **11 native cases** (0 failures/errors/skips). Local default Node 24 reports 17 passing file-isolated suites; explicit non-isolated execution confirms 649 individual cases. BUILD_STATUS.md records the exact separation of validation categories. The baseline had 481 individual JS cases, 5,368 browser assertions/11 layouts and 11 native unit cases with lint zero errors/12 warnings. New coverage protects writing uncertainty/false rejection, mode scoring, revisions, snapshots, assistance, B course completion, banks, table sources, paradigm alternatives, stage eligibility and permanent identities. Existing behavioural tests were retained; unavailable-stage assertions were deliberately updated while historical scopes/fixtures remain protected.

Local Chromium is blocked by sandbox file-navigation policy; rendered checks use established CI. No emulator, physical-device, acoustic TTS or native-speaker/educator test has been performed for this update. Synthetic single-voice audio, self-assessed speaking, a tiny placement screen, finite comprehension variants, conservative limited morphology, uncertain free writing and authored model coverage remain known limitations. These are visible product constraints, not automatic proficiency guarantees.

Technical debt: static bank/source duplication and a still-central session controller remain understandable but should be measured before refactoring. Broader dictionary/verb coverage needs browser packaging/licensing/testing. Review can preserve an older response snapshot intentionally; do not silently rewrite historical questions. Independent free-writing assessment remains a future design problem rather than a hidden unreliable classifier.

**Recommendation:** freeze expansion as a full-course **user-testing candidate**, not an unconditional classroom-certified release. Real learners and a Spanish educator should prioritise false rejects/accepts, ambiguous tasks, B-level pacing/length/depth, register, vocabulary load, synthetic-audio comprehension, assisted reflection and mobile interruption/upgrade. Use [USER_TESTING.md](USER_TESTING.md). Do not start C1/C2 or add another major feature while this test period is running.

Confirmed scope: all A1.1–B2.2 stages playable; old progress protected; deterministic constrained grading retained; valid open alternatives can survive as uncertain; Games do not complete course progress; grammar-level selection is independent; Tables remain randomised and Spain/vosotros-first. No microphone, ASR, cloud/LLM grader, accounts/backend, C1/C2 or formal CEFR certification was introduced.
