# Friends to Amigos Story Bible

Exactly three **primary** recurring characters: Ana, Luis and Marta. Diego, Clara, Elena and the other established names remain supporting characters. Existing role-play models and alternative ages in isolated exercises are not new biographical events. Stories are standalone episodes, not a chronological series.

Ana and Luis are friends; Marta is Luis’s sister and Ana’s friend. Romance is not a permanent relationship between the trio. Dates and other supporting adults can appear without creating required serial continuity. No episode assumes another episode was read. A callback must explain enough locally; “previously” notes are optional, never assessed memory.

## Editorial rules

- Setup → small complication → action → payoff. The answer to an inference must be supported by words on the page.
- Aim for familiar language carrying one manageable challenge. An 80–90% familiar editorial aim is not a measurable claim about a learner’s vocabulary.
- Practical / social / comedy episodes should be balanced across the **revised set**; audit the whole catalogue honestly. A tone label does not turn an ordinary passage into comedy.
- Humour comes from a recognisable misunderstanding. No jokes based on nationality, disability, learner accent, humiliation or dangerous health advice.
- A1 uses concrete chunks; A2 adds taught narration/plans; B1 adds interpersonal reasons; B2 adds viewpoint, implication, register and qualified evidence. Personalities remain constant.
- Use Spain-first vocabulary and vosotros/as where useful. Tú among friends; a service encounter may use usted, consistently and with a clear speaker.
- Dialogue/chat/review/email formats are plain local text. No external embeds. Five permanent question slots remain; controlled sequencing, perspective, summary and supplied-message construction vary the task. Optional predictions are ungraded and never create SRS/mastery evidence.
- Never change a saved passage or active question to the newest edition. New story text must not clear an older same-ID review merely because its answer is a common word.
